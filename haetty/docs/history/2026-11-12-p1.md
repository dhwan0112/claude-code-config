## P1a: `closed_sessions_leave_no_threads` — a real leak, not test timing

The Windows PC's check-range (`checks/windows-2026-11-11`) failed this test at `780b991` in 2 of 3 runs (WSL, low
memory). On kramers it failed 2 of 8 runs with four copies at once (Linux, `d229316`), always after the 30 s wait:

```
daemon threads 4 before, 6 after 60 sessions (0 still listed):
{"haetty": 1, "haetty-accept": 1, "haetty-client-1": 1, "haetty-pty-writ": 1, "haetty-sess-1a1": 1, "haetty-writer-1": 1}
```

A stuck run, caught while it waited: the daemon had **no child left** (the session's child was gone and reaped), its
`haetty-pty-writer` thread sat in `wait_woken` (a write to the PTY master waiting for room), and `haetty-sess-…` only
waited for its event channel, which the writer thread keeps open. So a closed session leaked two threads and its PTY
for good — not a timing problem of the test.

Cause, two parts:
- portable-pty's master writer (`UnixMasterWriter`) writes `"\n"` and EOF with a blocking `write_all` in `Drop`. The
  child of the flooding third of the test never reads its input; once that queue is full and the child is gone, the
  write waits forever (Linux does not wake a writer on the master when the child's side closes).
- `hangup()` (SIGHUP and `tcflush`, stage S4) runs only while bytes wait and only once: a write after the flush can
  fill the queue again. Under load the engine's end and the writer's next chunk race.

Fix (`haetty-pty/src/unix.rs`): haetty's own reader and writer on copies of the master descriptor; the master is
non-blocking; a read or write that would wait polls the master and a close pipe whose write end the session's control
holds. When the control is dropped (the engine ends), a waiting read returns end-of-file and a waiting write fails
(`BrokenPipe`), every master descriptor closes and the kernel hangs up the child's terminal — the usual terminal close
(SIGHUP to the child's session) instead of the `"\n"` + EOF portable-pty typed into the child. No timeouts, no
wakeups while idle. The engine no longer reports a write error after the session's end. Windows is unchanged (its
backend is its own; the test passes there with its slack).

Checks:
- New unit tests in `haetty-pty` (`unix::tests`): input and output pass; a writer blocked on a child that is gone is
  released by dropping the control (`BrokenPipe`), including its drop; a reader waiting on a silent child returns
  end-of-file and the child then exits by SIGHUP. With the old `unix.rs` (`d229316`) and the same tests: both release
  tests FAIL (writer still blocked after 5 s; reader still blocked). New code: 3 passed, three runs.
- `threads` test, 16 runs with four at once on the fixed build: 16 passed (3.7–3.9 s each); before: 2 of 8 FAIL.
- fmt; clippy `-D warnings` on `haetty-pty` and `haetty-core`, Linux (all targets) and `x86_64-pc-windows-gnu`; the
  gate before the commit.

## P1a: the tag rule

The person's rule, written into `CLAUDE.md` (Conventions), `docs/SELF-HOSTING.md` ("Dev → stable") and
`docs/dev-commands.md`: a tag needs the full Linux candidate check, a PASS of kramers' Linux night run and check-range
with 0 failing; the Windows night run does not hold it; the release's CHANGELOG section gets a "Windows:" line
(verified / unverified / known problems). Reason: the v0.3.1 candidate's Windows night passed once in three runs of one
build, stopped twice by the focus check.
