## P1a: `closed_sessions_leave_no_threads` — a real leak, not test timing

The Windows PC's check-range (`checks/windows-2026-11-11`) failed this test at `780b991` in 2 of 3 runs (WSL, low
memory). On kramers it failed 2 of 8 runs with four copies at once (Linux, `d229316`), always after the 30 s wait:

```
daemon threads 4 before, 6 after 60 sessions (0 still listed):
{"haetty": 1, "haetty-accept": 1, "haetty-client-1": 1, "haetty-pty-writ": 1, "haetty-sess-1a1": 1, "haetty-writer-1": 1}
```

A stuck run, caught while it waited: the daemon had **no child left** (the session's child was gone and reaped), its
`haetty-pty-writer` thread sat in `wait_woken` (a write to the PTY master waiting for room), and `haetty-sess-…` only
waited for its event channel, which the writer thread keeps open. So a closed session leaked two threads and its PTY
for good — not a timing problem of the test.

Cause, two parts:
- portable-pty's master writer (`UnixMasterWriter`) writes `"\n"` and EOF with a blocking `write_all` in `Drop`. The
  child of the flooding third of the test never reads its input; once that queue is full and the child is gone, the
  write waits forever (Linux does not wake a writer on the master when the child's side closes).
- `hangup()` (SIGHUP and `tcflush`, stage S4) runs only while bytes wait and only once: a write after the flush can
  fill the queue again. Under load the engine's end and the writer's next chunk race.

Fix (`haetty-pty/src/unix.rs`): haetty's own reader and writer on copies of the master descriptor; the master is
non-blocking; a read or write that would wait polls the master and a close pipe whose write end the session's control
holds. When the control is dropped (the engine ends), a waiting read returns end-of-file and a waiting write fails
(`BrokenPipe`), every master descriptor closes and the kernel hangs up the child's terminal — the usual terminal close
(SIGHUP to the child's session) instead of the `"\n"` + EOF portable-pty typed into the child. No timeouts, no
wakeups while idle. The engine no longer reports a write error after the session's end. Windows is unchanged (its
backend is its own; the test passes there with its slack).

Checks:
- New unit tests in `haetty-pty` (`unix::tests`): input and output pass; a writer blocked on a child that is gone is
  released by dropping the control (`BrokenPipe`), including its drop; a reader waiting on a silent child returns
  end-of-file and the child then exits by SIGHUP. With the old `unix.rs` (`d229316`) and the same tests: both release
  tests FAIL (writer still blocked after 5 s; reader still blocked). New code: 3 passed, three runs.
- `threads` test, 16 runs with four at once on the fixed build: 16 passed (3.7–3.9 s each); before: 2 of 8 FAIL.
- fmt; clippy `-D warnings` on `haetty-pty` and `haetty-core`, Linux (all targets) and `x86_64-pc-windows-gnu`; the
  gate before the commit.

### The short soak (`scripts/e2e/soak-short.sh`)

`soak.sh` unchanged in what it does; two numbers became parameters (`SOAK_LIMIT`, `SOAK_SAMPLE_S`). The verdict's
judged window is now half the run, at most 1800 s (`soak-verdict.py` and its Windows twin): 1800 s for the hour as
before, 300 s for 600 s.

The numbers:
- **600 s.** soak.sh scales the hour's operation counts by DUR/3600: 200/50/20/10 → 33 tab open/close, 8 detach and
  attach, 3 tasks, 2 theme changes (46 operations). The hour's operations take about 910 s for 280, so about 150 s
  here; soak.sh spreads them with about 7 s between and ends them about 2 min before the end.
- **Judged window 300 s** (half the run): the hour judges memory over at least 30 min after every tab is full; the
  short run keeps the same share. So every tab must be full within about 4 min of the start.
- **Scrollback limit 160 000 bytes.** The slow tab prints 5 rows/s; on haetty's engine a row of the soak costs about
  216 bytes (800 000 bytes held 3 703 rows in the hour runs), so 160 000 bytes hold about 740 rows, full after
  150–185 s. libghostty keeps its scrollback in pages: at 160 000 bytes it holds 350–730 rows (as at 800 000), full
  within the first minute. The burst tab (500 rows every 10 s) is full in about 30 s on both.
- **Samples every 30 s**: the full time to the half minute; about 20 samples.
- The rule is the hour's: threads, fds, descendants and sessions unchanged first to last, task worktrees 0, every
  operation done, alive; GUI and daemon anonymous memory at most 4 MB above their values at the full time.

### The daemon's health record

- `crates/haetty-daemon/src/health.rs`: the counts (Linux `/proc/self/task`, `/proc/self/fd` less the listing's own,
  `RssAnon`; Windows threads by a Toolhelp snapshot, `GetProcessHandleCount`, private bytes), the largest since the
  start, and the growth rule: three hourly lines in a row with more threads or descriptors (and neither fewer) at the
  same sessions and clients → one `health_warning` line (and a `warn` log line), again only after the run breaks.
  Clients count too: each has a reader and a writer thread, so a second window is not growth.
- The event loop: `recv_timeout(500 ms)` polled for the idle exit; now it waits for the next hourly line, or, with no
  session and no client, for the idle exit's time. The daemon has no other periodic wakeup. `idle_since` is taken at
  each event while busy, so the idle time counts from the last client's leaving, as before.
- `ClientMsg::GetHealth` / `DaemonMsg::Health(DaemonHealth)`, protocol 12. **Role table (protected, `Daemon::message`):
  `GetHealth` joins `ListSessions`' row — Observe and Control, not Report.** It carries no session content.
- `haetty status --json` gains `"health": {"now": {...}, "max": {...}, "started_ms", "started"}` (`null` without a
  daemon); a count the system did not give is `null`.
- `Config::health_every` (tests shorten it; the product uses `HEALTH_EVERY` = 1 h).

Checks: `health::tests` (4: the warning after three rises and once per run, sessions/clients/fall do not count, the
largest per field, Linux counts follow the process); `tests/daemon.rs` `health_counts_the_hourly_line_and_the_idle_exit`
